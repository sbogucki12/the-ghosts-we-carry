Featured Excerpt

"The Ghosts"

The ghosts follow him, though most people can't see them. They sit in the empty chair across from him at breakfast. They ride shotgun on long drives. They stand at the foot of his bed on sleepless nights. Not metaphorical ghosts. He's too literal-minded for that. These are the actual dead, preserved in his memory with high-definition clarity.

But most persistent are Kuhns and Kinslow. They visit him more frequently than the others, their presence particularly acute during his morning shower as he brushes his teeth. A peculiar habit he's maintained for years: the water cascading over his shoulders as he methodically cleans each tooth, feeling the shape of them under the bristles. The tactile sensation inevitably conjures thoughts of Kuhns, of the teeth that should have been in Ramadi instead of his own.

Frequently Asked Questions

About the Book

What is The Ghosts We Carry about?

The Ghosts We Carry: From Combat to the Disconnected Generation is a memoir by Iraq War veteran Steve Bogucki about combat, connection, and what it means to finally stay. It follows survivor's guilt, a lifelong pattern of walking away, the isolation of a generation more connected than ever and more alone, and the love that finally said: don't quit on me.

Is this a war memoir?

Not exactly. Combat is one of the book's three threads, alongside isolation and redemption, but this is not a war story. It's a story about what happens after: about patterns carved so deep they feel like fate, and the love that finally breaks them.

Is it a true story, and why is it told in the third person?

Yes, it is a true story. It is told in the third person simply because that is the style of writing I personally enjoy reading.

What is the “Pattern of Surrender”?

It is the book's name for a lifelong impulse to quit and walk away when things get hard. In the manuscript's words, “The Pattern of Surrender had defined him for forty-two years” (Part XV). When it speaks, it says: “Leave. Walk away. It's easier in the dark” (Part XVI).

What does “the Disconnected Generation” mean?

It describes millennial men who are more connected than any generation before them and more alone. As the book puts it: “Three men in three separate units, experiencing three variations of the same millennial condition: unprecedented connectivity coupled with unprecedented isolation” (Part VIII).

How is the book structured?

The book has a Prologue, sixteen parts, and an Author's Note. The parts are: Part I: Origins; Part II: Childhood and Early Formation; Part III: Warfare; Part IV: Ghosts and Guilt; Part V: Transitions; Part VI: Full Circle; Part VII: Special Forces Selection; Part VIII: Love and Loss; Part IX: Fire; Part X: Algorithms; Part XI: Rhythms; Part XII: Thresholds; Part XIII: Reconstruction; Part XIV: Breaking Point; Part XV: The Pattern of Surrender; Part XVI: Collision.

What difficult topics does it cover?

The book covers male loneliness, deep insecurities (specifically regarding my teeth), combat, drug and substance abuse, and sexual topics.
